How well are cities actually doing on sustainability — and how do we measure that rigorously? This work uses spatial analysis and multi-source data to assess urban sustainability across three interconnected dimensions: transport systems, public health infrastructure, and ecological and economic indicators.

On **sustainable transport**, we analysed the tidal flow patterns of dockless bike-sharing in Beijing, identifying the dominant temporal rhythms of shared bike demand across the city and their implications for system management and active travel planning ([Tan et al., 2022](#)).

On **health and urban accessibility**, we developed a hierarchical two-step floating catchment area (H2SFCA) method to model patient referral flows under different service configurations, contributing to evidence on equitable healthcare access within China's Healthy China 2030 initiative ([Xiao et al., 2021](#)). Separately, we examined the spatiotemporal spread of COVID-19 across Chinese cities, mapping how the epidemic diffused through urban networks at an early stage ([Wang et al., 2022](#)).

On **local sustainability indicators**, current work within the GALLANT programme (Glasgow as a Living Lab Accelerating Novel Transformation, funded by UKRI/NERC, NE/W005042/1) focuses on developing localised ecological and economic indicators to assess Glasgow's position against the Doughnut Economics framework — balancing social foundations with planetary boundaries. This work operationalises 44 definitions of 'the city' to examine how boundary choices affect sustainability assessments, and has produced the [Glasgow Digital Doughnut](https://glasgow-digital-donut-eqj2r3akt-gusss-projects.vercel.app/), an interactive tool tracking over 50 indicators from 2015 to 2024.
