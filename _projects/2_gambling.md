Gambling is a highly spatialised industry, yet its geography has received limited academic attention. My work examines where physical gambling venues are located across Great Britain, how that landscape has changed over time, and what consequences proximity to gambling venues has for individuals' online gambling behaviour.

This research was conducted during my postdoctoral position at the University of Bristol, supported by the **Bristol Hub for Gambling Harms Research**. It also includes work as co-lead of a Rapid Evidence Review commissioned under the **UKRI Research Programme on Gambling** (funded by the Arts and Humanities Research Council, AHRC): *Putting gambling harms in their place: A Rapid Evidence Review on gambling and its spatial footprint.*

**Key contributions:**

- Documented the spatial stability of gambling venues on British high streets between 2014 and 2023, showing that the presence of gambling venues is highly predictable from local area characteristics, with a predictive accuracy of 91.8% by 2019 — remaining robust at 86.2% in 2023 ([Li & Tranos, 2025](#))
- Used a **double negative control design** to estimate the causal effect of residential exposure to physical gambling shops on online gambling participation, finding that individuals in the highest exposure decile face approximately 1.49 times the risk of online gambling compared to those in the lowest decile ([Li et al., 2026](#))
- Compiled and published the **[Gambling Shop POI Dataset for Great Britain, 2014–2023](https://doi.org/10.5255/UKDA-SN-858518)**, the first longitudinal POI dataset of gambling venues in Great Britain, archived with the UK Data Service
