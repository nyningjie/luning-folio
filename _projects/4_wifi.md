**Beijing Normal University, 2016–2023.**  
Funded by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC).  
In collaboration with Shichahai Scenic Area Management, Beijing.

---

Urban historic districts attract millions of visitors each year, yet the fine-grained movement of tourists through these spaces is difficult to observe at scale. This work develops new methods to capture and interpret tourist mobility using **passively collected Wi-Fi probe data** — signals continuously broadcast by smartphones as they search for known networks. By deploying a network of sensors across Shichahai, one of Beijing's most-visited historic scenic areas, we recorded the presence and movement of hundreds of thousands of visitors at high spatial and temporal resolution, without requiring any active participation from tourists.

Working from this dataset, we addressed a series of methodological and substantive questions: How can raw device trajectories be translated into meaningful behavioural patterns? What are the dominant space–time rhythms of tourist flows across a scenic area? And how can visitor data be used to quantify the attractiveness of individual spaces and facilities?

**Key contributions:**

- Characterised the properties of Wi-Fi probe data as a source for human mobility research, and reviewed its applications in smart tourism and crowd sensing contexts ([Li et al., 2021a](#))
- Developed the **Semantic Space–Time Matrix (SSTM)** method to extract contextual meaning from movement trajectories, applied to data from 463,874 individuals over two weeks ([Li et al., 2021b](#))
- Proposed an **Empirical Orthogonal Function (EOF)** decomposition approach to identify dominant space–time patterns of tourist flows at the community scale ([Li et al., 2023](#))
- Introduced the **Stay-Time-based Tourism Attractiveness Index (STTAI)**, using one month of data from 670,000 travellers to quantify the relative draw of different spaces within the scenic area ([Li et al., 2024](#))
